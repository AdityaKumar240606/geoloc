1. Data Clustering (Finding the "Places")
Right now, your database only knows latitudes and longitudes. A model cannot easily predict a raw coordinate. You first need to convert raw dots into labeled places (Points of Interest).

The Approach: Pull your historical data from PostgreSQL into a Python environment. Use libraries like Pandas and NumPy to group coordinates where your speed dropped to zero for more than 15-20 minutes.

The Output: Instead of [87.3025, 22.3210], the data becomes Place_ID_1 (which you can manually label as "Classroom" or "Cafe").

2. Feature Engineering (Adding Context)
A location alone isn't enough to predict the next location. The model needs context. For every visit to a place, you extract:

Day of the week: (e.g., Monday)

Time of day: (e.g., 10:00 AM)

Duration of stay: (e.g., 45 minutes)

Previous location: (e.g., Dorm)

3. Choosing the Right Model
You do not need a massive neural network for this. You can start with lightweight, highly effective algorithms:

Markov Chains: This is the easiest starting point. It calculates simple transition probabilities. For example, it looks at your history and sees that 90 out of 100 times, going from "Dorm" at 9:00 AM on a weekday leads to "Class".

Decision Trees / Random Forests: As your data grows, these models can handle more complex rules (e.g., "If it is Tuesday at 1:00 PM and I just left the lab, where do I go next?").

4. Serving the Predictions
Once the model is trained in Python, you can wrap it in a fast, lightweight API using a framework like FastAPI. Your frontend tracking app can ping this API with your current location and time, and the API will return the percentage probabilities for your next likely destinations.
